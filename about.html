ABOUT / CORE CONCEPT

What is Gradari Mireris?

The project combines strategic play with character-driven roleplay. At the strategic level, players can influence governments, factions, economies, territory, fleets, armies, colonies, diplomacy, infrastructure, intelligence, and research. At the character level, players inhabit people who exist inside those systems and are limited by their own authority, location, knowledge, relationships, and responsibilities.

The point is not to choose from a fixed list of game actions. If something is reasonably possible inside the setting, a player can attempt it. Larger ambitions usually require greater resources, preparation, political authority, cooperation, or risk.

The Core Cycle

Observe → Plan → Act → Resolve → Record → Adapt.

Players examine the current situation, decide what they want to accomplish, act through roleplay or formal Actions, and receive a resolution from the Game Master. Meaningful outcomes enter the continuing Record and become part of the world that everyone must respond to next.

Persistent Consequences

There is no routine reset back to a default board state. Territory can change hands. Resources can be spent. Governments can rise or fracture. Treaties can become binding. Characters can gain authority, lose influence, or die. Research and discoveries can permanently change what a faction knows or is capable of doing.

Characters

Characters let players experience the setting at a human scale. Each player maintains one active living character at a time. Characters can die, deceased characters remain part of the Record, and a new character can enter play afterward. A character does not automatically know everything the player knows, and influence must come through the authority and access that character actually possesses.

Senate Politics

Factions can hold seats in the Senate, vote as political blocs, pay Imperial taxes to the Gradari Mireris Empire, and sponsor legislation through characters with political authority. Characters build and spend Influence as their personal political capital.

Formal Actions

When an attempt needs Game Master resolution, players submit a formal Action. A useful Action explains four things: the Objective, the Method, the Resources being committed, and the Intent behind the attempt.

The Game Master

The Game Master represents uncontrolled factions and characters, maintains hidden information, applies canon, resolves Actions, and determines how the setting responds. Players determine what they attempt. The Game Master determines the consequences and the resulting world state.
