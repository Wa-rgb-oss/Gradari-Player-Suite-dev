PROJECT BRIEF // GRADARI MIRERIS

PERSISTENT SCI-FI STRATEGY + ROLEPLAYTHE LIVING
RECORD

Gradari Mireris is a persistent science-fiction strategy, roleplaying, and 4X project where players act inside a shared universe whose political, military, economic, diplomatic, and personal consequences continue forward.

ABOUT / CORE CONCEPT

What is Gradari Mireris?

Gradari Mireris is a persistent strategy and roleplaying game built around a living science-fiction setting. Players operate at two scales at once: the strategic scale of governments, economies, territory, institutions, and war, and the personal scale of characters who actually live inside that world.

At the strategic level, players can build industries, control resources, participate in markets, develop colonies and systems, establish political organizations, influence governments, conduct diplomacy, pursue research, manage military forces, gather intelligence, compete for territory, and shape the institutions of the Gradari Mireris Empire.

At the character level, those systems are experienced through individual people. A character has a location, position, relationships, responsibilities, access to information, and limits on what they can realistically command. A wealthy industrialist, Senator, military officer, researcher, colonial administrator, diplomat, or political organizer may all interact with the same world very differently.

The game is not built around choosing actions from a predetermined menu. The systems provide structure, resources, limits, and consequences, but they are not intended to define everything a player is allowed to attempt. If something is reasonably possible within the setting, a player can pursue it.

That may mean forming a commercial enterprise, negotiating a treaty, investigating a political rival, financing an expedition, influencing an election, establishing a colony, organizing a military operation, developing a new technology, attempting espionage, sponsoring legislation, manipulating a market, or pursuing something the game has never encountered before.

The larger the ambition, the more the world pushes back. Resources, authority, preparation, location, intelligence, alliances, infrastructure, timing, and risk all matter. Being able to imagine an action does not guarantee the ability to accomplish it.

The Core Cycle

Gradari Mireris revolves around a continuing cycle of information, decisions, consequences, and response. Players examine the world around them, decide what they want to accomplish, commit resources or authority, act through the game systems or roleplay, and then respond to the consequences of what happened.

Some goals can be handled directly through the game's systems. Others require cooperation, negotiation, roleplay, or a formal Action submitted to the Game Master. When an outcome requires interpretation, uncertainty, opposition, or consequences that cannot be handled automatically, the attempt is resolved by the Game Master.

Important outcomes enter the continuing Record. A successful expedition may reveal something previously unknown. A failed military operation may weaken a faction. A treaty may alter political relationships. A character's death may create a succession crisis. A new industrial network may change the economic balance of an entire region.

The world after an action is not always the same world that existed before it. Players are expected to respond to those changes and make their next decisions from the situation that now exists.

A Persistent World

Gradari Mireris does not routinely return to a default board state. The campaign exists as a continuing historical record. Decisions accumulate over time, and consequences can remain relevant long after the original action that caused them.

Territory can change hands. Systems can be developed. Infrastructure can be built or destroyed. Markets can shift. Governments can rise, reform, fracture, or collapse. Alliances can become institutions. Wars can permanently reshape political boundaries. Resources can be exhausted or redirected. Characters can build reputations that continue to influence events years later.

A decision made during one part of the campaign may become the political problem another player inherits much later. This persistence also means that power is rarely static. A faction that dominates economically may be politically weak. A military power may lack industrial independence. A small organization may control a strategically important location. A character with little formal authority may possess information capable of changing an election or destabilizing an alliance.

The game is intended to create history rather than simply determine winners and losers.

Players and the Empire

Every player exists somewhere within the broader political world of the Gradari Mireris Empire, but that does not mean every player must belong to the same political organization or player faction.

Players may join or establish factions, cooperate with existing powers, pursue independent economic interests, participate in Imperial politics, or operate primarily through their character. A faction is therefore not the player's identity. It is one of the institutions a player may participate in.

This distinction allows characters and players to exist within the Empire without requiring everyone to approach the setting through territorial conquest or faction leadership. One player may become deeply involved in Senate politics while another builds an industrial network. Another may focus on research, exploration, intelligence, military service, commerce, diplomacy, or character-driven roleplay.

These paths can overlap, but they do not have to.

Characters

Characters allow players to experience the setting at a human scale. Each player maintains one active living character at a time. That character exists physically within the world, occupies locations, interacts with other people, develops relationships, acquires authority, and experiences the consequences of political and strategic events.

Characters are not omniscient representations of the player. A character does not automatically know everything visible to the player or everything discussed outside of roleplay. Information must reach the character through believable means, such as personal observation, public information, reports, communication, intelligence networks, political access, allies, or other characters.

Likewise, knowing what should be done does not mean a character has the authority to do it. A Senator may influence legislation but have no authority over a military fleet. A naval commander may control powerful forces but lack the ability to rewrite Imperial law. A corporate executive may possess enormous financial resources without holding political office. A researcher may understand technology that powerful political figures do not.

Authority, knowledge, and access matter.

Characters can also die. Death is part of the persistent Record rather than a temporary inconvenience. Deceased characters remain part of the history of the setting, and the player may create a new character afterward. The replacement character begins with their own position, relationships, knowledge, and circumstances rather than automatically inheriting everything possessed by the previous character.

Economy and Industry

The economy of Gradari Mireris is intended to function as more than a scoreboard for accumulated currency. Players can acquire Aureum, resources, infrastructure, production facilities, storage capacity, and industrial capabilities.

Mines extract raw materials. Refineries process them. Factories transform refined resources into more advanced goods. Agricultural infrastructure supports food production. Research facilities generate scientific progress. Storage and commercial infrastructure allow larger economic networks to operate.

Facilities also create obligations. Construction requires capital. Operating infrastructure creates upkeep. Expanding production can create storage problems, resource shortages, logistical dependencies, and exposure to changing market conditions.

The Guilded Concord Exchange allows players to buy and sell resources, but market activity can also influence the value of goods. A resource that is highly profitable today may become less valuable as supply increases or demand falls.

Economic growth is therefore intended to create new decisions rather than simply making earlier decisions irrelevant.

Research

Research represents long-term technological and institutional development. Research Sites generate Research Points, which players can invest into a connected network of advancements. Early research establishes foundational capabilities, while later tiers require previous discoveries before they can be unlocked.

Research may improve industrial production, logistics, storage, agriculture, manufacturing, research efficiency, infrastructure, military technology, or other systems as the network expands.

Not every player is expected to develop identically. Over time, research should allow players to specialize. One player may build an advanced industrial economy while another develops logistics, science, political institutions, or military technology.

Research therefore becomes part of a player's long-term identity within the campaign rather than simply another resource to accumulate.

Factions

Factions allow groups of players to organize around shared political, economic, military, or territorial goals.

A faction can represent far more than a traditional nation-state. It might be a political bloc, noble House, corporate organization, colonial movement, military institution, regional government, ideological organization, commercial alliance, or another organized body capable of exercising influence.

Factions can maintain shared resources, control territory, develop systems, hold infrastructure, participate in diplomacy, organize military forces, and compete for political influence.

They can also disagree internally. Membership in the same faction does not require characters to share identical goals. Political factions can contain rival personalities, competing interests, and internal power struggles. Leadership may delegate authority, alliances may shift, and internal decisions may become just as important as conflicts with outsiders.

The faction system exists to coordinate players, not to replace individual character agency.

Senate Politics

The Imperial political system gives players another arena in which power can be exercised. Factions can hold seats in the Senate, organize into political blocs, support legislation, compete for offices, and influence the direction of Imperial policy.

Characters with the appropriate political authority can sponsor legislation, participate in elections, negotiate support, and use personal Influence as political capital. Influence represents a character's ability to turn relationships, reputation, access, political favors, and institutional standing into real political leverage.

Economic power and political power therefore remain related without being identical. A wealthy faction may possess enormous resources but limited Senate representation. A smaller political organization may control enough seats to become essential to passing legislation. Individual characters may gain substantial influence even when their faction does not dominate the wider Empire.

Imperial politics are intended to create negotiation, coalition-building, rivalry, compromise, and opportunities for players who are interested in power without necessarily pursuing territorial conquest.

Diplomacy

Agreements between players matter because the world remembers them.

Diplomacy may involve trade arrangements, military cooperation, territorial agreements, research partnerships, political coalitions, guarantees, intelligence sharing, access rights, ceasefires, alliances, or agreements unique to the circumstances of the campaign.

Not every diplomatic interaction requires a formal treaty, but agreements that become part of the Record can carry consequences. Breaking an agreement may be possible. It may also damage trust, trigger retaliation, undermine a character's reputation, destabilize a government, or reshape future negotiations.

Diplomacy works because other players are not scripted opponents. They remember what happened.

Warfare

Military conflict is part of the setting, but warfare is not intended to exist separately from the political and economic systems around it.

Military forces require resources, infrastructure, leadership, logistics, intelligence, and strategic objectives. Controlling territory does not automatically mean being able to defend it. Possessing a fleet does not guarantee knowing where an enemy will attack.

A war may be decided by industrial capacity, political alliances, intelligence failures, logistics, preparation, geography, technology, or leadership long before opposing forces directly engage one another.

Military operations can also create consequences far beyond the battlefield. A victory may destabilize a government. A failed campaign may destroy a political career. A blockade may reshape markets. A costly war may leave both sides weaker than the powers that remained neutral.

Warfare is another part of the continuing world rather than an isolated combat minigame.

Information and Imperfect Knowledge

Information is one of the most important resources in Gradari Mireris.

Players may know things their characters do not. Characters may possess intelligence that other characters cannot see. Governments and factions may hide their intentions. The Game Master may maintain information that no player currently possesses.

This creates an important distinction between what is true, what is known, and what is believed.

Characters act based on the information available to them. A military operation based on incorrect intelligence may fail even if the player understands the mistake afterward. A political conspiracy may remain effective because its participants successfully concealed their involvement. A discovery may become valuable precisely because very few people know about it.

The setting becomes more interesting when uncertainty matters.

Formal Actions

Many activities can occur directly through roleplay or through the automated systems of the game. When an attempt requires Game Master resolution, players submit a formal Action.

A useful Action explains four things: the Objective, what you are trying to accomplish; the Method, how you are attempting to accomplish it; the Resources, what people, money, equipment, authority, intelligence, infrastructure, or other assets are being committed; and the Intent, what outcome you are actually trying to create.

The quality of an Action is not determined by how long it is. A short Action with a clear objective, realistic preparation, strong resources, and a sensible method may be far more effective than several pages of description that do not explain how the attempt is supposed to work.

The Game Master evaluates the situation surrounding the Action, including available resources, preparation, technology, terrain, information, opposition, leadership, timing, previous events, and other factors relevant to the attempt.

The result does not always have to be simple success or failure. An Action may succeed at a cost, partially succeed, reveal new information, produce unintended consequences, create a new opportunity, or make the situation considerably worse.

The Record

The Record is the accumulated history of the campaign. Important political changes, conflicts, treaties, discoveries, deaths, territorial changes, major Actions, and other significant developments become part of the setting's continuing history.

The Record serves both a practical and narrative purpose. Practically, it establishes what has already happened and prevents the setting from quietly resetting when circumstances become inconvenient. Narratively, it allows the campaign to develop its own history.

A political rivalry that begins between two characters may eventually become a conflict between entire institutions. A small colonial dispute may become the origin of a major war. A discovery made by one researcher may reshape industry generations later.

The campaign is intended to become increasingly defined by the people playing it.

The Game Master

The Game Master represents everything that does not belong directly to the players. This includes uncontrolled governments, political organizations, military forces, corporations, characters, institutions, hidden information, environmental conditions, and other forces within the setting.

The Game Master also maintains canon, adjudicates formal Actions, interprets situations that automated systems cannot resolve, and determines how the wider setting reacts to player decisions.

The Game Master is not there to determine what players are allowed to want. Players decide what they attempt. The Game Master determines how the world responds.

That distinction is central to Gradari Mireris. The goal is not to guide players toward a predetermined story. The goal is to maintain a coherent world capable of responding to whatever story the players create.

PLAYER GUIDE // HOW TO PLAY

PERSISTENT STRATEGY + ROLEPLAYHOW TO
PLAY

Gradari Mireris is played by making decisions inside a persistent world. The systems establish resources, limits, authority, time, and consequences. Players decide what they attempt, and the world responds.

HOW TO PLAY / INTRODUCTION

The Three Scales of Play

Strategic Play concerns governments, factions, economies, territories, fleets, armies, colonies, diplomacy, infrastructure, research, and the movement of power across the galaxy. Decisions at this scale can alter entire systems.

Roleplaying Play concerns the individuals who live inside those systems. Commanders, governors, soldiers, diplomats, investigators, merchants, scientists, officials, and other characters may negotiate, investigate, fight, explore, form relationships, or pursue ambitions of their own.

Narrative Play connects the two. Political decisions can create wars. Wars can create shortages, scandals, heroes, political crises, and new opportunities. A conversation between two characters may eventually alter Imperial policy. A failed expedition may expose something capable of changing the balance of a region.

No scale exists entirely separate from the others.

The Core Gameplay Cycle

Most play begins with the current state of the world. Players examine what is happening, decide what they want to accomplish, determine what resources or authority they can use, and act through the game systems, roleplay, diplomacy, or a formal Action.

Some results are handled automatically by the site. Others require the Game Master to determine what happens. Once an outcome is established, meaningful changes become part of the continuing Record and affect what is possible next.

There is no permanent reset between sessions. The consequences of play accumulate.

Player Agency

Players are not limited to a menu of predetermined actions. If something is reasonably possible within the setting, a player may attempt it.

You may establish industries, negotiate agreements, conduct espionage, investigate events, organize political movements, build infrastructure, move military forces, sponsor research, influence markets, establish colonies, pursue office, or attempt something that the game has never encountered before.

The existence of an action does not guarantee its success. Larger ambitions usually require greater preparation, resources, authority, cooperation, time, or risk. The rules define consequences more often than they define permission.

There Is No Protected Story

Gradari Mireris does not follow a predetermined script. Important characters may die. Political institutions may collapse. Wars may be lost. Alliances may fracture. Research may create consequences that were never anticipated.

Canon establishes the setting that exists when play begins. Player decisions and Game Master resolutions determine what becomes true afterward.

Players, Characters, and Factions

Every player exists within the wider political world of the Gradari Mireris Empire. A player may also belong to a faction, participate in an institution, pursue independent economic interests, hold political authority, or focus primarily on a character.

A faction is an organized power capable of pursuing objectives inside the game world. Factions may control territory, maintain shared resources, participate in politics, conduct diplomacy, organize military forces, develop infrastructure, and compete for influence.

A player does not directly control every person inside an organization. Authority allows the player or character to issue decisions, but logistics, information, subordinate personnel, opposition, morale, politics, and circumstance still influence the result.

Characters

Each player maintains one active living character at a time. That character is the player's direct personal presence in the setting and remains limited by their location, authority, knowledge, relationships, and responsibilities.

Players may know more than their characters know. Information discovered elsewhere does not automatically become character knowledge. Classified intelligence, private conversations, distant events, and hidden developments must reach the character through a believable path before they can act upon them.

Characters can die. A deceased character remains part of the Record, and the player may create a new character afterward. The new character enters play with their own position, relationships, knowledge, and circumstances.

Actions and Orders

The primary method for attempting something that requires Game Master resolution is a formal Action. Actions can be personal, political, economic, military, diplomatic, investigative, or strategic.

A character might question a prisoner, arrange a private meeting, investigate an installation, negotiate with another official, or attempt to escape an ambush. A larger Action might establish a colony, deploy a fleet, begin a research project, impose sanctions, conduct an intelligence operation, fortify a system, or prepare an invasion.

Writing an Action

A strong Action normally explains four things.

Objective: What are you trying to accomplish?

Method: How are you attempting to accomplish it?

Resources: What personnel, Aureum, equipment, territory, political authority, intelligence, infrastructure, or other assets are being committed?

Intent: What result do you actually want from the attempt?

Preparation matters. Intelligence gathered before an operation, infrastructure built earlier, political relationships developed over time, available technology, logistics, and previous decisions can all change the likelihood and consequences of an Action.

The scale of the Action should match the scale of the change being attempted. A player can pursue an enormous goal, but a goal such as conquering a major region may require a series of military, political, logistical, and economic decisions rather than resolving through a single submission.

Action Resolution

The Game Master resolves an Action from the conditions that actually exist at the time of the attempt. Resources, preparation, technology, terrain, information, leadership, timing, opposition, and previous decisions may all influence the result.

An Action may succeed, partially succeed, fail, succeed at a cost, reveal information, produce an unexpected consequence, or create another situation that requires a decision.

The purpose of resolution is not to reward or punish a player. It is to determine how the world reasonably responds to what was attempted.

Economy and Aureum

Aureum is the primary currency used to support player activity. It represents the financial capacity to maintain infrastructure, fund construction, operate institutions, trade, expand industry, support military forces, and pursue other objectives.

Players can earn and spend Aureum through the systems available to them. Facilities can produce resources, refineries and factories can develop those resources further, and the Guilded Concord Exchange provides a market for buying and selling goods.

Economic decisions create obligations. Facilities have construction costs and upkeep. Production requires storage. Expanding too quickly can create dependencies or leave a player unable to support everything they have built.

Expansion and Development

Players and factions may develop systems through facilities, infrastructure, industry, research, population growth, political investment, military construction, and other persistent assets.

Expansion is not limited to claiming new territory. Strengthening an existing system, creating an industrial center, improving logistics, establishing research infrastructure, or fortifying a strategically important location can be just as important.

Diplomacy and Politics

Players may negotiate alliances, trade arrangements, territorial settlements, political coalitions, guarantees, ceasefires, research partnerships, intelligence agreements, and other arrangements that make sense within the setting.

Imperial politics also operate through Senate representation, political offices, legislation, Influence, taxation, relationships, and the authority of individual characters. Political power and economic power are related, but they are not the same thing.

Warfare

Warfare depends on more than the number of military units involved. Fleets, armies, defensive positions, supply, infrastructure, technology, leadership, intelligence, geography, timing, and political objectives all matter.

Capturing territory does not automatically make that territory secure. Military victories can create occupation problems, economic disruption, political instability, resistance, diplomatic consequences, and new strategic obligations.

Technology and Research

Research Sites generate Research Points that can be invested into the Research Network. Research advances are connected by prerequisites, so higher tier advancements require the linked research beneath them to be unlocked first.

Research can improve industry, logistics, storage, agriculture, manufacturing, scientific development, military technology, or other systems as the network expands. Specialization allows different players to develop different long term strengths.

Time and the Record

Gradari Mireris uses the Aevum Calendar and a persistent world clock. One real world hour corresponds to five in-universe hours. One in-game day contains thirty-six hours.

Construction, research, production, travel, politics, warfare, and other systems can continue as time advances. Important events become part of the Record so later decisions can be resolved against a consistent history.

What happened remains true until something within the game changes it.

The Game Master

The Game Master maintains everything beyond direct player control. This includes uncontrolled factions and characters, hidden information, world events, canon, and the consequences of Actions.

The Game Master does not determine the story players must follow. Players decide what they attempt. The Game Master determines how the setting responds.

Beginning Play

Start by understanding your character, your available resources, your current location, the political situation around you, and the systems you can meaningfully influence. You do not need to understand every part of the galaxy before taking part.

Read the current news and world state, examine the Map and Player Suite, decide what your character or organization wants, and begin making decisions. Some goals can be pursued directly through the site. Others can be handled through roleplay or submitted as formal Actions.

The campaign should begin with enough information to make meaningful decisions, not with months of preparation before anyone is allowed to act.
